Topic A: Working with Numbers

Order of operations, negatives, and number lines.

01 — Order of Operations

Doing Steps in the Right Order

When a problem has many steps, always do them in the same order. Remember it as PEMDAS.

  • P — Parentheses: do what's inside ( ) first.
  • E — Exponents: powers and square roots next.
  • MD — Multiply / Divide: left to right, whichever comes first.
  • AS — Add / Subtract: left to right, whichever comes first.
  • Negatives: a negative times a negative is positive. −3 × −4 = 12.
Example: 3 + 4 × 2 = 3 + 8 = 11 (not 14, because multiply comes before add).

02 — Number Line & Absolute Value

Negatives & Absolute Value

Numbers get bigger as you move right on a number line.

  • Comparing: −8 is less than −2, because −8 is farther left.
  • Absolute value |x|: how far a number is from 0. It is never negative.
  • Adding a negative: same as subtracting. 5 + (−7) = −2.
  • Subtracting a negative: same as adding. 5 − (−7) = 12.
Example: |−9| = 9 and |4| = 4, so |−9| is bigger.

03 — Fractions & Decimals

Fractions, Decimals & Converting

Fractions and decimals are two ways to write the same amount.

  • Add/subtract fractions: make the bottoms (denominators) the same first.
  • Multiply fractions: top × top, bottom × bottom.
  • Divide fractions: flip the second fraction, then multiply.
  • Fraction → decimal: divide top by bottom. 3/4 = 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75.
  • Decimal → percent: move the decimal two places right. 0.75 = 75%.
Example: 1/2 + 1/3 = 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6.

04 — Exponents & Roots

Exponents, Roots & Scientific Notation

An exponent tells you how many times to multiply a number by itself.

  • Exponent: 2⁴ = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 16.
  • Square root √: what number times itself gives this? √49 = 7.
  • Anything to the 0 power: equals 1. 5⁰ = 1.
  • Scientific notation: a number from 1 to 10 times a power of 10. 4,500 = 4.5 × 10³.
Example: 3² + √16 = 9 + 4 = 13.
