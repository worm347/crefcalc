01 — Mean, Median, Mode & Range

Mean, Median, Mode & Range

These four numbers describe a list of data.

  • Mean: the average. Add them up, divide by how many.
  • Median: the middle number after putting them in order. If two middles, average them.
  • Mode: the number that shows up most.
  • Range: biggest minus smallest.
Example: 3, 5, 5, 8, 9 → mean 6, median 5, mode 5, range 6.

02 — Charts & Graphs

Reading Charts & Graphs

The GED loves bar graphs, line graphs, circle graphs, and tables. Read the title and labels first!

  • Bar graph: compares groups.
  • Line graph: shows change over time.
  • Circle (pie) graph: shows parts of a whole; the parts add to 100%.
  • Scatter plot: shows if two things are related (positive, negative, or no trend).
Example: A pie chart shows 25% of 200 students walk to school. 0.25 × 200 = 50 students.

03 — Simple Probability

Simple Probability

Probability is how likely something is, from 0 (never) to 1 (always).

  • Probability: P = (ways you want) ÷ (total ways).
  • As a percent: 1/4 = 0.25 = 25%.
  • Not happening: P(not A) = 1 − P(A).
  • Fair coin: P(heads) = 1/2. Fair die: P(any one number) = 1/6.
Example: A bag has 3 red and 7 blue marbles. P(red) = 3/10.

04 — Counting & Combined Events

Counting & Combined Events

When two things happen, multiply to count all the ways.

  • Counting principle: 3 shirts and 4 pants make 3 × 4 = 12 outfits.
  • Independent events: one doesn't change the other. P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B).
  • Permutation: order matters (1st, 2nd, 3rd place).
  • Combination: order doesn't matter (picking a team).
Example: Flip a coin twice. P(two heads) = 1/2 × 1/2 = 1/4.
